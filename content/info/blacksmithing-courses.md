# Learning at our shop

We don't offer formal instruction or classes at the moment. The best way to get started with us is to come out to a [shop day](/events/). There's almost always someone around who's happy to show you how to light and manage a coal fire, get you comfortable with a hammer, and walk you through a simple first project. It's informal, everyone learns at their own pace, and you'll pick up a lot just by watching the other smiths.

If it's your first time, have a look at [what to bring to your first meet](/info/first-meet/) and [how to find our shop](/info/how-to-find-us/) before you come.

# If you want formal classes

If you'd rather have structured lessons, here are a few options we recommend:

### Iron Sunset Forge

Henrietta, one of our guild members, teaches blacksmithing classes at [Iron Sunset Forge](https://ironsunsetforge.com/). Check her website for upcoming class dates.

### NAIT Continuing Education

NAIT in Edmonton runs a series of part-time blacksmithing courses at its main campus, starting with [IRON101 Blacksmithing](https://www.nait.ca/nait/continuing-education/courses/iron101-blacksmithing), where you make your own punch, hammer and tongs. From there you can go on to intermediate blacksmithing, a course on axes and hatchets, or a five-day blacksmithing bootcamp. See the [full list of NAIT blacksmithing courses](https://nait.ca/coned/blacksmith-courses). Classes tend to fill up, so register early.

### Front Step Forge

Shawn Cunningham has run [Front Step Forge](https://frontstepforge.com/) in Edmonton since 1992, and he's the instructor for the NAIT courses above. His [classes page](https://frontstepforge.com/pages/classes) has the latest on what he's teaching and when.
